## Requirements for Thailand (2026)

Philippine passport holders enter Thailand **visa-free** for tourism. What to prepare:

- **Visa-free stay** — up to 60 days under the current rules (raised from 30 in mid-2024). Confirm the limit before you go, since it has changed recently.
- **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)** — the mandatory online arrival form that replaced the old paper card. File it within 72 hours before arrival at [tdac.immigration.go.th](https://tdac.immigration.go.th) and keep the confirmation for immigration.
- **eTravel QR code** — the Philippine departure (and return) registration. Fill it within 72 hours of each flight at [etravel.gov.ph](https://etravel.gov.ph).
- **Boarding pass & booking reference** — needed at check-in; Thai immigration can also ask for proof of onward or return travel, so keep your return ticket handy.
- **Passport valid at least 6 months** beyond your travel dates.
- **Philippine travel tax** — ₱1,620 full economy rate. On Cebu Pacific it is usually bundled into the fare (ours was — see below).

Entry rules change often — double-check the visa-free window and the TDAC process before you book.

## Where to Stay

For a group, I'd book a **two-bedroom condo near MRT Sutthisan** (Blue Line). It's a short metro ride to Chatuchak, Sukhumvit, and the Pratunam shopping area, and the surrounding Huai Khwang neighbourhood has cheap local food and night markets.

The one we stayed at was around 358 Soi Sabai Chai 13, Khwaeng Samsen Nok, Khet Huai Khwang, Bangkok 10310. Search that area on Airbnb.

## What It Cost

**Estimated total: around ₱40,000** per person for the four days — I didn't itemise this trip, sorry. What I can pin down: our **round-trip Cebu Pacific fare from Clark was ₱12,630.40**, which already included 20 kg of checked baggage and the Philippine travel tax. The rest went to the group condo, the dinner cruise, Ancient City and Icon Siam, shopping around Pratunam, Grab rides, and food.

Bangkok is forgiving on a budget once you're there — street meals are cheap, the MRT and BTS are cheap, and Grab is affordable split across a group.

## Final Thoughts

Four days is enough for a first taste of Bangkok if you keep it tight: one shopping day, one for the big sights and a river cruise, and one for temples before the flight home. Flying out of Clark kept the airfare low, and splitting a two-bedroom condo near the MRT kept accommodation cheap. Go in with your TDAC and eTravel already filed, some baht in hand, and a Grab account set up.
