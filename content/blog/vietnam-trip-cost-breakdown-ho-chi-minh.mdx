## Requirements for Vietnam (2026)

Good news first: Philippine passport holders travel to Vietnam **visa-free** for tourism, for stays of up to 21 days. What you actually need to prepare:

- **eTravel QR code** — this is the Philippine departure registration, not a Vietnam requirement. Fill it out within 72 hours (3 days) before your flight at [etravel.gov.ph](https://etravel.gov.ph) and save the QR code for check-in and immigration.
- **Flight booking reference** — airlines ask for it at check-in, and Vietnam immigration can ask for proof of onward or return travel, so have your return ticket handy.
- **Passport valid at least 6 months** beyond your travel dates.
- **E-SIM** — I booked mine through [Klook](https://www.klook.com/en-PH/kreatorshop/IVYKLK027KLOOK/) (Agoda also sells them). Install it before you fly and activate on landing.
- **Philippine travel tax** — ₱1,620 full economy rate, payable via TIEZA online or at the airport.
- **Some cash** — bring a bit of USD to exchange; rates in the city (District 1) beat the airport.

Entry rules change — confirm the current visa-free window and documents before you book.

## What It Cost

**Estimated total: ₱30,000–₱35,000** per person for the 5 days — flight, hotel in District 1, the Mekong Delta and cruise tours, food, coffee, and some shopping. Saigon is easy on the budget: street meals run cheap, Grab rides are short and inexpensive, and most of the District 1 sights are walkable or a few minutes apart. Rough exchange rate at the time: ₫10,000 ≈ ₱23.

## Final Thoughts

Ho Chi Minh City is one of the easiest, cheapest trips you can do from Manila — short flight, visa-free, walkable District 1, and food that barely dents the budget. Two big tours (the Mekong Delta and the river cruise) plus daily coffee, shopping, and meals still landed comfortably in the ₱30,000–₱35,000 range per person. If you split a hotel with friends the way we did, it goes lower.
