## Requirements for Korea (2026)

Philippine passport holders still need a tourist visa for South Korea — there's no visa-free entry or K-ETA shortcut for us. Here's what to budget and prepare:

- **Tourist visa application via KVAC** — filed through the Korea Visa Application Center (KVAC) in Taguig.Visa runs about ₱1,000(Updated since August 25 2026). I already held a multiple-entry visa from a past trip, so this cost me nothing this time.
- **Supporting documents** — passport valid at least 6 months, accomplished application form, ID photo, proof of employment or ITR, bank certificate plus 3–6 months of statements, and a travel itinerary. Frequent travellers to OECD countries may qualify for simplified requirements. (If you have qualified card i suggest go through Simplified process so you do not need to present ITR and bank certificates)
- **Philippine travel tax** — ₱1,620 full economy rate, payable online via TIEZA or at the airport. I paid ₱1,700 with the processing add-on online. (can find online site in [https://etravel.gov.ph](https://etravel.gov.ph))
- **Round-trip airfare, Manila ↔ Incheon** — budget carriers land around ₱10,000–₱15,000 on promo, ₱18,000–₱25,000 in regular season. January is winter peak, but fares right after New Year can dip.
- **Korea Electronic Arrival Card** — fill this out online before you fly at [e-arrivalcard.go.kr](https://www.e-arrivalcard.go.kr), the official site. It replaces the old paper arrival card.

Rates shift constantly — treat these as a planning baseline and confirm the current numbers before you book.

## Grand Total

**₱61,322** — a working estimate, with a little bit of shopping folded in. Cut the souvenir splurge on Jan 22 and you're comfortably under ₱50,000 for a solo winter week in Seoul. You'll save when you have a roommate when choosing a hotel or go to hostel if you want the cheapest rates.

## Final Thoughts

Seoul in January is cold but genuinely rewarding — the ice festivals are once-a-year, the museums are free, and convenience-store food keeps the daily spend low. My biggest budget lever was accommodation: splitting a room or booking a hostel would knock several thousand pesos off this total. Go in with a Namane Card loaded, a rough itinerary, and layers you actually trust, and a solo Seoul winter trip is very doable on a mid-range budget.
