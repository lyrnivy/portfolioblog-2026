## How Hot Does It Actually Get?

In July, Osaka and Kyoto experience intense summer heat and heavy humidity, with average daytime highs reaching 30°C to 35°C (86°F to 95°F) and peak temperatures often climbing past 38°C to 40°C (100°F+).

**Osaka:** Average highs range from 30°C to 34°C (86°F to 93°F), with record highs nearing 39°C.

**Kyoto:** Average highs range from 29°C to 35°C (84°F to 95°F), with record highs reaching nearly 40°C. Kyoto often feels slightly more trapped in its basin, amplifying the stagnant heat compared to Osaka.

It's not just the number on the thermometer — the humidity is what makes it exhausting to walk around all day.

## What to Wear

Bring flowy, loose-fitting clothes. Linen or any lightweight, breathable fabric that doesn't cling is a lifesaver once the humidity sets in — anything tight or synthetic will have you drenched by midday. A tiered maxi skirt like this is exactly the kind of flowy, breathable silhouette that works:

## Cooling Gear: What to Buy Before You Go vs. What to Buy in Japan

**Bring from home:**

- Cooling mist spray
- Sunscreen
- A UV-blocking umbrella

**Buy once you're there:**

- Neck cooling rings (ice rings) — sold at nearly every konbini and drugstore
- Portable handheld fans

Having an eSIM ready the moment you land makes it easier to pull up maps and weather apps on the spot rather than hunting for shade while you figure out wifi. I use **Airalo** for this — my code is in the deals box below.

## Avoid Being Outdoors From 12–3 PM

This is the single biggest tip: as much as possible, don't stay outdoors between noon and 3 PM. It's the hottest stretch of the day, and we saw elderly visitors suffer heatstroke during this window. Plan indoor stops — museums, department stores, cafes — around these hours and save outdoor sightseeing for morning or after 4 PM.

That's also roughly when I caught [Tenjin Matsuri](/blogs/japan-trip-cost-breakdown-osaka-kyoto) in Osaka — the land procession and fireworks happen in the evening, after the worst of the heat has passed, which made the festival itself much more bearable than wandering around at midday.

## Final Thoughts

Summer in Osaka and Kyoto is genuinely intense — the heat and humidity catch a lot of visitors off guard, especially around matsuri season when you want to be outside for the festivities. Pack light, breathable clothes, grab cooling gear as soon as you land, and build your day around avoiding the 12–3 PM peak. Do that, and the heat becomes a manageable part of the trip instead of the thing that ruins it.
